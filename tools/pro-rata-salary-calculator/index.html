How pro-rata pay is calculated

Pro rata means "in proportion". Two proportions can apply at once, and the calculation multiplies both:

pay = full-time salary × (your hours ÷ full-time hours) × (days worked ÷ 365)

The two reductions are independent

Someone working 24 hours against a 40-hour full-time week is on 60% FTE. If they also only work seven months of the year, they receive 60% of the salary for 7/12 of the year — about 35% of the full-time annual figure. Confusing these two reductions, or applying only one, is the most common payroll error in part-time contracts.

Mid-month joiners and leavers

Employers use one of two conventions and they give different answers:

MethodHow it worksEffect
Calendar-dayAnnual ÷ 365 × days employedUsed here. Neutral across months.
Working-dayMonthly ÷ working days in that month × days workedSlightly favours joiners in short months

If your contract or local law specifies one, use that. The difference is usually small but it is real, and it is worth checking on a final settlement.

What is normally pro-rated too

Annual leave entitlement, bonuses and in many countries end-of-service benefits are all pro-rated on the same FTE basis. Part-time employees are generally entitled to the same proportion of benefits, not a reduced rate of pay per hour — paying a lower hourly rate purely for being part-time is unlawful discrimination in much of the world.

Frequently asked questions

What does pro rata actually mean on a job advert?

It means the advertised salary is the full-time figure and you will be paid the proportion matching your hours. A role advertised at 60,000 pro rata for 3 days a week actually pays about 36,000.

How do I calculate pro-rata annual leave?

Multiply the full-time entitlement by your FTE percentage. At 60% FTE with a 25-day full-time allowance, you get 15 days. If you work fixed days, it is usually cleaner to count leave in hours.

Should part-time staff get a lower hourly rate?

No. In most jurisdictions, paying a lower hourly rate solely because someone is part-time is unlawful. The total is lower because the hours are fewer, not because the rate is.

Which is right for a mid-month start — calendar days or working days?

Both are used. Calendar-day is the more common default and the one applied here. Check your contract, since the two methods can differ by a few percent in a given month.

Does pro rata affect end-of-service or gratuity?

Usually yes. Most systems base end-of-service on the actual salary paid and the length of service, so a part-time salary produces a proportionally smaller benefit. Country rules vary — see the country calculators.
