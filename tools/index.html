Why these tools exist

Calcnate started with country-specific employment calculators — end-of-service, gratuity, notice periods and leave across the Gulf, South Asia and beyond. These global tools are the layer underneath: the money and time arithmetic that is the same wherever you work.

Each one is built around three principles:

  • Show the working. Every tool prints the formula and a breakdown, not just a number, so you can check it against your own payslip, contract or bank statement.
  • No hidden assumptions. Working weeks, weekend patterns, compounding frequency and withdrawal rates are all yours to set. Tools that hard-code a 40-hour week and a Saturday–Sunday weekend quietly produce wrong answers for most of the world.
  • Say what is excluded. Tax, fees and local variations are named explicitly rather than silently ignored.

Frequently asked questions

Are these calculators free?

Yes, all of them, with no sign-up and no limit on use. They run entirely in your browser.

Do they work in my currency?

Yes. Every money tool has a currency selector covering more than thirty currencies, and the arithmetic is currency-neutral, so an unlisted currency works too — the label is cosmetic.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. All calculations run locally in your browser. Nothing you type is transmitted to a server or stored.

Can I use these for official or legal purposes?

They are estimates for planning and checking, not legal, tax or financial advice. For anything binding — a settlement, a tax return, a mortgage offer — confirm with the relevant authority or a qualified professional.

How accurate are they?

The arithmetic is exact and each page states its method and assumptions. Accuracy for your situation depends on the inputs and on local rules the tool cannot know, which is why every page lists what it excludes.
